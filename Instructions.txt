# Austin Davis — Immersive Writer / Storyteller Website

Build a premium, cinematic personal website for **Austin Davis**, a writer, poet, storyteller, musician, and emerging speaker.

The website is not a generic writer portfolio and should not feel like a motivational-coach website.

The purpose is to introduce Austin's writing and eventually build an audience around a larger message:

> **We spend so much of our lives learning how to survive, achieve, produce, and move forward that sometimes we forget to actually live.**

Austin is exploring what it means to live a meaningful, present, human life.

The site should feel like entering a story.

It should be **quiet, immersive, emotional, sophisticated, cinematic, literary, and extremely polished.**

Do NOT make it feel corporate, trendy, SaaS-like, overly animated, or like a template.

---

# DESIGN DIRECTION

Use the supplied hero image as the primary visual foundation.

The hero represents a rural Alabama road where Austin's family lived and spent time together.

The road is metaphorical:

**The visitor is beginning a journey through Austin's memories, stories, questions, and ideas.**

The site should feel like the visitor is slowly walking down that road.

Think:

* cinematic documentary
* literary journal
* independent film
* old photographs
* handwritten memories
* Southern landscape
* warm nostalgia
* quiet sophistication
* modern editorial design

Avoid:

* generic motivational imagery
* excessive animations
* gradients everywhere
* glassmorphism
* glowing UI
* tech aesthetics
* oversized buttons
* stock-template layouts
* excessive rounded cards

The site should feel timeless.

# TYPOGRAPHY

Use exactly these fonts:

### Primary display serif

**Cormorant Garamond**

Use for:

* hero headline
* story titles
* major quotes
* emotional statements
* section headings

Use font weights 400 and 500 primarily.

### Italic display

**Cormorant Garamond Italic**

Use selectively for emotional emphasis.

Example:

"remembering how to live."

### UI / navigation / metadata

**Inter**

Use for:

* navigation
* buttons
* labels
* metadata
* newsletter form
* small descriptions

Use weights 400, 500, and 600.

### Handwritten accent

Use **Brittany Signature** or a similar restrained handwritten font ONLY for occasional signature-style elements.

Do not use the handwritten font for normal body text.

Typography should be elegant and restrained.

# HERO ANIMATION

On initial load:

1. Image fades from black over approximately 1.5 seconds.
2. Navigation fades upward into position.
3. Eyebrow fades in.
4. Main headline fades upward.
5. Supporting copy follows.
6. Scroll indicator appears last.

Use staggered animation.

Total entrance sequence approximately 2 seconds.

Use:

cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)

Animations should feel cinematic rather than snappy.

---

# SCROLL EXPERIENCE

This is extremely important.

The website should feel like the visitor is moving through a memory.

Do NOT use excessive scroll-jacking.

Never prevent normal browser scrolling.

Use subtle scroll-linked animation.

As the user scrolls away from the hero:

* hero image slowly scales from 1.0 to approximately 1.08
* image subtly darkens
* foreground content moves slightly slower than the scroll
* hero text fades naturally
* road remains visually present as long as possible

Use CSS transforms and IntersectionObserver where possible.

Avoid heavy JavaScript scroll calculations.

Performance is extremely important.

# IMPORTANT MESSAGE

The website should communicate this philosophy without turning it into a manifesto:

Austin is not presenting himself as someone who has figured life out.

He is exploring it.

The underlying message:

We are constantly told to become more.

More successful.

More productive.

More accomplished.

But somewhere along the way, many of us forget to simply be.

Austin writes about family, childhood, ambition, time, freedom, loss, memory, love, music, and the question:

**What does it actually mean to live?**

The site should invite the visitor to explore that question with him.

Never preach.

Never tell visitors that they are "asleep" or that Austin has "the answers."

The tone should be humble:

**"I'm trying to understand this too."**

# PAGE TRANSITIONS

When navigating between pages, use subtle fade transitions.

---

# BUTTON DESIGN

Buttons should be minimal.

No giant filled CTA buttons.

Primary style:

uppercase Inter

letter-spacing: 0.15em

font-size: 11–13px

thin 1px border

generous horizontal padding

transparent background

On hover:

* background becomes warm cream
* text becomes dark
* transition approximately 400ms

Arrow moves approximately 6px.

---

# MICRO-INTERACTIONS

Use micro-interactions everywhere, but keep them restrained.

Examples:

* navigation underline slowly expands on hover
* arrows move slightly
* images subtly scale
* text reveals upward when entering viewport
* rules draw themselves across the page
* photographs move 2–4px relative to surrounding content
* handwritten elements appear slowly

Nothing should bounce.

Nothing should flash.

Nothing should feel like a Web3 website.

---

Body:

18px desktop

17px mobile

---

# PERFORMANCE

The site must feel exceptionally smooth.

Target:

60fps interactions.

Avoid:

* large JS animation libraries unless absolutely necessary
* scroll event listeners firing continuously
* excessive DOM elements
* huge unoptimized images
* autoplay video
* unnecessary dependencies

Use:

* CSS transforms
* opacity
* IntersectionObserver
* requestAnimationFrame only when necessary
* lazy loading
* responsive image sizes
* WebP/AVIF where appropriate

Respect:

prefers-reduced-motion.

When reduced motion is enabled, disable parallax and complex entrance animations.

# MOST IMPORTANT DESIGN PRINCIPLE

The site should make someone feel:

**"I don't know what this is yet, but I want to keep going."**

The road is the metaphor.

The visitor begins at the road.

They discover the memories.

They discover the writing.

They discover the questions.

Eventually, they discover the larger philosophy.

Do not explain everything on the homepage.

Leave room for mystery.

The website should feel like the beginning of a book.

Not the conclusion.

---

# FINAL QUALITY BAR

Before considering the implementation finished, ask:

Does this look like a $10,000–$20,000 custom editorial website?

Does it feel like Austin Davis has a unique voice?

Does the website feel emotional without being cheesy?

Does it feel premium without being pretentious?

Does it feel personal without being cluttered?

Does the road actually feel like the beginning of a journey?

Would someone who knows nothing about Austin want to scroll?

Would someone who reads one story want to read another?

If the answer is no, refine it before stopping.

Do not settle for "technically complete."

The goal is **memorable.**
